What this page will focus on?

This page will focus on how your training can influence recovery, and how I setup people's trainnig to ensure they can recover properly. Arugably more important than what you're doing outside of the gym in terms of recovery because we can see training like the dial that we turn up or down in order to amplify or reduce training dose (the total amount of work done in a session or multiple sessions). When we increase training dose we will inherently increase the recovery demand too, sometimes when we do this we are simply asking too much from ourselves, even when our nutrition, sleep and stress management are perfect.

So, there of course has to be a limit of how much training you can do and deal with. This is why training is not as simple as more = better, it's actually the balance between stimulus and being able to recover from the fatigue produced by that stimulus = results.

Signs of not being recovered

Recovery can actually be defined as the ability for your performance to come back to baseline, which is a really good way of seeing whether or not you have recovered. For example, normally you bench press 100kg x 4 reps, but now you only do 2 reps, this is a sure sign you aren't recovered (unless you just took a break from the gym).

Signs you're not recovered go much deeper than this though, and include:

Performance from one set drops massively from the other

For example, set 1 you get 8 reps, set 2 only 4-5, set 3 only 3-4. This suggests your body is not fully recovered from previous training sessions, and is a sign something in your training needs to change.

You feel tired in your day to day life

If you're feeling tired during the day for no good reason, this is a sign you are not recovered.

Joints, muscles and tendons ache and feel tired

This is a really big sign that you are not recovered, because tissues are still damaged and inflammation is going to those tissues, which is what happens during recovery, they need a break.

You feel low mood, and are just moody/negative about things in general

Obbviously this can be a sign of other things, but it can also be a sign you are not recovered, especially when combined with the other things mentioned.

Reduced appetite

A reduction in appetite can be a sign you aren't recovered, this is generally a sign of long term under-recovery.

Poor sleep

Bad sleep is a sign of poor recovery, but may be confusing because isn't sleep supposed to be important for recovery? Well, this is why modulating your training is so important.

Volume and its effects on recovery

Volume, which we can define as sets per week can be split up into minimal effective dose (MED), maximal adaptive volume (MAV), maximal recovery volume (MRV). MRV would be what we are mainly talking about here, most people's MRV tends to be around 20+ sets per week, per body part, doing more than this might lead to inadaquate recovery and stalling progress. So it's a good idea to ensure you do not do more than this, here's the caveat though... Some people will be able to recover from more than others, this is due to genetic differences, how well your lifestlye is setup to improve recovery. This will also change as you lift for longer, as each set you do will be different as you get stronger (you'll produce more fatigue per set). Minimum effectve dose tends to be around 8 sets per week for most people, for most muscles.

How can we manipulate volume to improve recovery? We can push volumes high for just 2-3 weeks adding a little more volume per week until we finally pull back, this allows us to get an adaptation from the increase in volume, but also allows us to recover because we are pulling back from the high volume. This enables many weeks and months to be stacked on top of one another, where it builds and builds.

If you ever notice yourself feeling tired in day to day life, feeling demtoivated to train, feeling weak in the gym, getting bad pumps, low mood - all at once. This tends to suggest you are having problems with recovery, and it's a sign that you could do with a reduction in volume. This may mean doing 2 sets instead of 3-4 sets for just a week, personally I tend to prefer a reduction in intensity as I feel I notice a bigger difference when I reduce my intensity rather than volume, for me I am more sensitive to intensity, whereas some people can be the complete opposite and be more sensitive to high volumes.
